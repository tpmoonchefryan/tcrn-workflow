# TCRN Workflow 1.0.0

`1.0.0` is the first accepted release. It is not a rewrite: the storage format,
the event shapes and every successful command's reason code are unchanged from
`0.14.0`. What changed is that the engine now carries a batch primitive, a
retrieval surface that answers in a workspace being written to, and a set of
gates that measure things nothing measured before.

It transforms no event, keeps `storageVersion` at 1, removes no verb, and
requires no `recover`. An engine at `0.14.0` and one at `1.0.0` read the same
chains.

## Why this is the version that gets the number

The prior releases were building the mechanism. This one closes the six things
the platform was built to do, and each has a measurement rather than a claim.
The accounting is on the chain as `TCRN-CROSS-REL-MVP-V1`.

Two of those measurements were failures first, and are worth stating here
because the fixes are what this release actually is.

**Retrieval was fully dark and nothing noticed.** All six governed partitions
refused every knowledge read with `KNOWLEDGE_HIGH_WATER_MISMATCH`, because the
store's marker had to equal the chain head exactly and *any* event moves the
head. One `work-create` was measured taking the whole retrieval surface offline
until a hand-run rebase (`TCRN-CROSS-INC-226`). The five metadata-first read
verbs now accept `--allow-trailing true` and label the answer with both heads,
so a stale card is admitted and disclosed rather than refused and invisible.

**A brief that named a bounded field did not carry its bound.** Ten dispatch
briefs each demanded four literal strings inside a field with a 512-byte ceiling
none of them stated; four of ten results exceeded it and were refused by the
store they were written for (`TCRN-CROSS-INC-235`). The control arm — a frontier
model on three of the same tickets — exceeded it on three of three, at 608, 625
and 848 bytes. Writing more thoroughly violated the unstated ceiling *more*,
which is what settled that the brief was the variable rather than the model tier.
Briefs may now declare `fieldBudgets` (`TCRN-CROSS-INC-236`). Re-running the same
tickets with that one variable changed, against an unmoved threshold and a
stricter scorer, gave 10 of 10 with zero budget violations in either arm
(`TCRN-CROSS-INC-237`).

## What is new

**`work-batch`** applies an ordered, heterogeneous sequence of work-record writes
as one act: one lease, one CAS decision, one clock reading, one view projection,
one time-attestation receipt. Measured on the same records, forty single verbs
take 1,651 ms with forty leases and forty CAS decisions; one batch of forty takes
51 ms with one of each. Members may reference each other by `externalKey` and
`parentExternalKey`, which is what makes "open an Epic and the Stories beneath
it" expressible at all.

**`knowledge-batch`** does the same for the knowledge store, with one difference
stated in its own module header rather than discovered later: it is per-member
atomic, not all-or-nothing. The store is a disposable, rebuildable projection,
and member-level rollback would mean building a transaction layer for a surface
the chain regenerates. Partiality is reported structurally — what landed, what
failed with its own reason code, what was never judged — as three lists a caller
cannot conflate.

**Four gates that measure what nothing measured.** A Markdown link gate, after a
published code of conduct spent a day telling readers the authoritative text was
at a path that did not exist, through every existing gate. An acceptance-verdict
leg, so "nobody ran it" cannot read as "green". A chain-headroom leg reporting
against a 15,000 review trigger rather than the ceiling. A field-budget check on
dispatch briefs.

**`maxChainEvents`** separates the chain's lifetime event bound from the
protocol's per-document shape bound. They shared one constant by an accident of
naming; raising it to buy chain headroom would have loosened six unrelated input
bounds. Replay was measured linear at 117 microseconds an event across a
seventeen-fold range, so 20,000 costs about 2.34 s per materialize against 0.51 s
at 4,316.

## Upgrading

Replace the engine copy. No `recover`, no migration, no chain touched. Verify
with `commands` against the copy you actually invoke — this repository's own
rule, and the one most often skipped.

## What this release does not claim

Goal 3 is demonstrated *for briefs that declare their field budgets*. A brief
naming a bounded target field without its bound is the round-one condition, and
round one failed.

Git-to-chain cross-linking sits at 43.5% with no enforcing gate. The correct
checkpoint is the `done` transition and needs chain-side design; a gate added in
haste would be either permanently red or hollow.

`TCRN-CROSS-INC-234` is open. The AOS repository imports a module this engine
retired in `0.11.18`; it was moved out of the acceptance criterion by Owner
ruling rather than repaired, and the roster carries the restoration condition.
